Data lineage as a graph — making "where did this number come from?" answerable

On PowerMe — a data catalog, governance & lineage platform — I owned two capabilities end to end: the data catalog and the lineage & impact engine, which I started as its founding engineer. This was an early-stage startup that scaled fast during my time there.

The problem

Enterprises couldn't trace how data flowed from creation (OLTP) to consumption (BI/OLAP). When a report looked wrong, nobody could say which upstream system, transformation, or table was responsible — across RDBMS, NoSQL, BI tools and viz platforms.

The insight

Lineage isn't tabular — it's a graph. Model tables, jobs and reports as nodes and data-flow as edges, and "trace this metric to its source" becomes a graph traversal, not a pile of joins.

What I owned

Data catalog

Cataloging the enterprise's data assets — datasets, tables, ETL jobs and the dashboards built on them — into one place teams could search and discover what data existed and where it lived, rather than hunting through tribal knowledge.

Lineage & impact

The capability I started as founding engineer. Model every asset as a node and each data-flow as an edge, so you can trace a metric back to its OLTP origin — and run impact analysis the other way: if this table or job changes, what downstream reports break?

How metadata got in — two paths

PULL System-specific adapters

Purpose-built connectors — one per source system. Each connects to its source, extracts the metadata, ingests it into PowerMe, and creates or updates the graph in the graph store. Adapters spanned every layer of the data landscape: OLTP, OLAP, ETL, the data-virtualization layer and BI systems.

PUSH Change-log listeners

Listeners that tail source-system change logs and push changes into PowerMe over Kafka — fully automatic, no manual involvement. As sources changed, the lineage graph stayed current on its own.

Both paths converge on the graph lineage store — pull to onboard a system, push to keep it live.

Beyond the build, I directed the end-to-end system design of the lineage engine and mentored engineers on graph databases and distributed systems.
